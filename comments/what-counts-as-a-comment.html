What counts as a comment

10 November 2026 · #meta

A paper takes months, often years, and along the way many ideas never make it into one. Some are too small, some too early, some simply do not fit the format of a journal article. This page is where they go.

Here, a comment is anything shorter and freer than a paper, but written with the same care.

In practice, a comment can take one of several forms:

  • A thought: an idea I am turning over, a question I cannot answer yet, or a hunch worth writing down before it fades.
  • A reflection on the state of the art: where a literature stands, what we know, and what we keep taking for granted.
  • A note on a new advancement: a paper, a method, a dataset or a piece of software that changes how I think about my work, and why.
  • General considerations on academia: doing a PhD, peer review, conferences, teaching and the everyday mechanics of research.
  • A short research article: a small analysis, a replication or a result too narrow for a journal but possibly useful to someone.

A few ground rules

Comments are not peer-reviewed, and they are provisional by design. I may change my mind, and when I do I will say so rather than quietly rewrite the past.

I write them first of all for myself: writing is how I find out what I actually think. But if one of them is useful to you, or if you disagree, I would be glad to hear about it at mattia.galante-1@unitn.it.
